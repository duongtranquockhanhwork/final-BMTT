1Correct use: RSA-PSS and ECDSA P-256

Sign a contract, then tamper with one character or use an impostor's key. Every modification is rejected, which shows integrity and authentication.

2Attack: ECDSA nonce reuse

A buggy signer reuses the nonce k. From two public signatures the attacker recovers k and the private key d, then forges an invoice that the library accepts.

3Defense: RFC 6979 deterministic nonces

The same attack pipeline against the patched signer: r never repeats, the recovered key is wrong and the forgery is rejected.

4Bonus: textbook RSA vs RSA-PSS

Multiplying two textbook RSA signatures gives a valid signature on the product. PSS padding blocks it.
